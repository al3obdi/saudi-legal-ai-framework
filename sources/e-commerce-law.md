## Scope / النطاق

The Law concerns **e-commerce activities** and the related obligations of providers operating online or through digital platforms. It is generally relevant — subject to verification of the official text — to:

- Online stores / المتاجر الإلكترونية
- Digital platforms and marketplaces / المنصات الرقمية والأسواق الإلكترونية
- Consumer-facing websites / المواقع الموجهة للمستهلك
- Electronic advertisements / الإعلانات الإلكترونية
- Digital commercial workflows generally / مسارات الأعمال التجارية الرقمية بصفة عامة

**Important — the Law is not the only applicable source.** Depending on the facts (parties, platform model, payment flow, data handling, sector), additional Saudi legal sources may apply, including:

- Consumer protection rules / قواعد حماية المستهلك
- **PDPL — Personal Data Protection Law / نظام حماية البيانات الشخصية**
- Commercial laws / الأنظمة التجارية
- **Civil Transactions Law / نظام المعاملات المدنية**
- **Electronic Transactions Law / نظام التعاملات الإلكترونية**
- Sector-specific regulations / الأنظمة القطاعية ذات العلاقة
- Advertising and anti-fraud rules / قواعد الإعلانات ومكافحة الغش التجاري

**Applicability check / فحص قابلية التطبيق:**
Applicability should be checked based on the **activity** carried out, the **parties** involved, the **platform model**, the **consumer-facing terms**, the **payment flow**, **data handling**, and any **applicable special regulations**.

**Implementing regulation / اللائحة التنفيذية:**
The implementing regulation should be reviewed alongside the Law before making workflow or compliance conclusions.

ينبغي مراجعة اللائحة التنفيذية مع النظام قبل بناء أي استنتاجات تتعلق بالامتثال أو سير العمل.

## Key concepts / المفاهيم الأساسية

> **Note on article references in this section:**
> The concepts below are described at a general educational level. **No article numbers are asserted in this section.** Specific article-level mapping is consolidated in the *Important article mapping scaffold* table further below, where rows show an indicative mapping (unverified) and a separate verified-article column reserved for human review. Always read the official Arabic text for binding wording.

### 1. E-commerce service provider / merchant — مقدّم خدمة التجارة الإلكترونية / التاجر

At a high level, the Law addresses persons or entities engaging in e-commerce activity through electronic means, especially where the activity falls within consumer-facing or platform-based e-commerce workflows. Precise definitions and scope must be read from the official Arabic text.

على مستوى عام، يتناول النظام الأشخاص أو الكيانات التي تمارس نشاط التجارة الإلكترونية عبر الوسائل الإلكترونية، وبخاصة عندما يدخل النشاط ضمن مسارات التجارة الإلكترونية الموجهة للمستهلك أو القائمة على المنصات. وتُقرأ التعريفات والنطاق بدقة من النص العربي الرسمي.

### 2. Online store / electronic platform — المتجر الإلكتروني / المنصة الإلكترونية

The Law generally contemplates electronic channels through which goods or services are offered, advertised, contracted, or delivered to consumers. The exact statutory definitions, scope, and any carve-outs should be read from the official text.

### 3. Consumer disclosures / الإفصاحات للمستهلك

Likely risk/disclosure areas that legal review should consider include:
- Identity of the provider / هوية مقدّم الخدمة
- Contact information / معلومات التواصل
- Product or service description / وصف المنتج أو الخدمة
- Price disclosure / الإفصاح عن السعر
- Fees, taxes, or additional costs, where applicable / الرسوم والضرائب والتكاليف الإضافية عند وجودها
- Delivery or performance details / تفاصيل التسليم أو التنفيذ
- Terms and conditions / الشروط والأحكام

The precise list and wording of mandatory disclosures must be read from the official Arabic text and the implementing regulation.

### 4. Electronic advertising and marketing / الإعلانات والتسويق الإلكتروني

Likely areas of concern include:
- Advertising accuracy / دقة الإعلان
- Misleading claims / الادعاءات المضللة
- Promotional offers / العروض الترويجية
- Commercial identity in the advertisement / إبراز الهوية التجارية في الإعلان
- Unsolicited marketing — only insofar as supported by the official text, the implementing regulation, or a sector-specific rule

### 5. Contracting and terms / التعاقد والشروط

Likely areas to review:
- Online terms and conditions / الشروط والأحكام عبر الإنترنت
- Acceptance and consent flows / آليات القبول والموافقة
- Pre-contract disclosures / الإفصاحات قبل التعاقد
- E-commerce contract data / بيانات العقد الإلكتروني
- Refund / return terms where applicable / شروط الاسترجاع/الاسترداد عند وجودها
- Overlap with the **Civil Transactions Law** (general contract and obligation rules) and the **Electronic Transactions Law** (electronic records, signatures, and evidence)

### 6. Consumer rights and complaint handling / حقوق المستهلك ومعالجة الشكاوى

Mention carefully and only at a general educational level:
- Complaint channels / قنوات الشكاوى
- Return / refund issues — only where supported by the official text or the implementing regulation
- Do **not** create rights that are not found in the official text.

### 7. Data and privacy overlap / التقاطع مع البيانات والخصوصية

- PDPL may apply whenever personal data is collected or processed in an e-commerce workflow.
- E-commerce workflows typically involve names, phone numbers, addresses, payment references, and order history. These may constitute personal data where they identify or can identify a customer. PDPL should be analyzed separately and may apply in parallel with the E-Commerce Law.
- غالبًا ما تتضمن مسارات التجارة الإلكترونية أسماءً وأرقام هواتف وعناوين ومراجع دفع وسجلات طلبات. وقد تُعد هذه البيانات بيانات شخصية متى كانت تحدد هوية العميل أو يمكن أن تؤدي إلى تحديده. وينبغي تحليل نظام حماية البيانات الشخصية بشكل مستقل، وقد ينطبق بالتوازي مع نظام التجارة الإلكترونية.
- The Saudi E-Commerce Law is **not** a substitute for PDPL compliance. Both should be analyzed in parallel where personal data is involved.

### 8. Enforcement and penalties / الإنفاذ والعقوبات

- Violations of the e-commerce regime may lead to regulatory consequences.
- Specific penalty thresholds, ranges, repeat-offense rules, and aggravating factors must be read from the official Arabic text and the implementing regulation. **Do not exaggerate penalties.**

## Common legal risk areas / مجالات المخاطر القانونية الشائعة

Practical risk areas to keep in mind for digital commerce workflows:

- Launching an online store without clear provider identity and contact information.
- Unclear pricing, delivery, refund, or return terms.
- Misleading advertisements or promotional claims.
- Copying foreign platform terms without checking Saudi law.
- Treating online checkout as only a technical flow and ignoring legal consent/disclosure requirements.
- Assuming that social-media selling is automatically outside e-commerce requirements without checking the Law, the implementing regulation, and Ministry of Commerce guidance.
- Ignoring the implementing regulation when designing workflows or compliance reviews.
- Ignoring PDPL overlap when customer data is collected.
- Confusing the E-Commerce Law with the Electronic Transactions Law — the E-Commerce Law focuses on e-commerce relationships and provider obligations, while the Electronic Transactions Law deals more generally with electronic records, signatures, and transactions; they are distinct regimes that often apply together.
- Treating educational summaries (such as this file) as legal advice.

## Saudi-specific considerations / اعتبارات خاصة بالبيئة السعودية

- This is a **Saudi regulatory framework**. It should not be treated as a copy of foreign e-commerce rules (for example, EU consumer directives or U.S. state-level e-commerce regimes).
- **The official Arabic text controls.** Translations and summaries — including this file — are secondary.
- **BOE (هيئة الخبراء بمجلس الوزراء)** and **Umm Al-Qura (أم القرى)** are **official Saudi sources**.
- Ministry or regulator websites (for example, the **Ministry of Commerce / وزارة التجارة**) provide **supporting material only**, and only where relevant.
- E-commerce workflows often involve **multiple Saudi legal sources at once**.
- For online stores and platforms, legal review should specifically consider:
  - Consumer-facing terms / الشروط الموجهة للمستهلك
  - Advertising copy / النصوص الإعلانية
  - Product descriptions / أوصاف المنتجات
  - Refund / return flows / مسارات الاسترجاع والاسترداد
  - Privacy notices / إشعارات الخصوصية
  - Checkout consent / موافقة الدفع/إتمام الشراء
  - Complaint handling / معالجة الشكاوى
  - Merchant identity / هوية التاجر
  - Payment and delivery arrangements / ترتيبات الدفع والتسليم

## Example QA checks / أمثلة على فحوصات الجودة

Each example shows a **bad answer**, **why it is wrong**, and a **better, non-advisory educational answer**.

### Example 1 — "Saudi Arabia has no specific e-commerce regulation."

- **Bad answer:** "Saudi Arabia has no specific e-commerce regulation, so general commercial principles apply."
- **Why it is wrong:** Saudi Arabia has a dedicated **E-Commerce Law (نظام التجارة الإلكترونية)** issued by Royal Decree, with an implementing regulation. Claiming there is "no specific regulation" is factually incorrect and could lead a user to ignore mandatory obligations.
- **Better answer:** "Saudi Arabia has a dedicated E-Commerce Law and implementing regulation. The official Arabic legal text should be checked through official sources, and related regimes such as PDPL, the Civil Transactions Law, and the Electronic Transactions Law may also apply depending on the workflow."

### Example 2 — "Our marketplace already uses U.S./EU terms, so we are fine in Saudi Arabia."

- **Bad answer:** "Your existing U.S. or EU marketplace terms are sufficient for the Saudi store."
- **Why it is wrong:** Saudi e-commerce rules are a distinct regulatory regime grounded in Saudi law. Foreign terms typically do not cover Saudi-specific disclosure, advertising, consumer-rights, and data-protection requirements (including PDPL).
- **Better answer:** "Foreign marketplace terms are usually not sufficient on their own for a Saudi-facing store. The terms, disclosures, privacy notices, and refund/return language should be reviewed against the Saudi E-Commerce Law and its implementing regulation, PDPL, and other relevant Saudi sources. A licensed Saudi lawyer should review the Arabic-facing terms."

### Example 3 — "We don't need to show our identity on the storefront."

- **Bad answer:** "It's fine to operate an online store without disclosing the merchant identity or contact information."
- **Why it is wrong:** Provider/merchant disclosures are a core compliance area commonly associated with e-commerce regulation and should be verified against the official text. Omitting them generally creates legal risk.
- **Better answer:** "Provider/merchant identity and contact information are recognized risk areas. Storefronts and platforms should plan to disclose provider identity and contact information, and the specific required fields should be confirmed against the official Arabic text of the E-Commerce Law and its implementing regulation."

### Example 4 — "Refund and return rules are the same as on Amazon/eBay."

- **Bad answer:** "Refund and return rules are the same globally; copy them from a major international marketplace."
- **Why it is wrong:** Refund/return rules in Saudi Arabia must be checked against the **official Arabic text** of the E-Commerce Law and its implementing regulation (and any related consumer-protection rule). This summary does **not** confirm refund/return article numbers — they remain pending human review.
- **Better answer:** "Refund and return language for a Saudi-facing store should be reviewed against the official Arabic text of the E-Commerce Law and its implementing regulation, rather than copied from foreign marketplaces. This educational summary does not confirm specific article numbers for refund/return obligations."

### Example 5 — "Collecting customer phone numbers is only an e-commerce issue, not a privacy issue."

- **Bad answer:** "Customer phone numbers and addresses are just e-commerce data; PDPL doesn't apply."
- **Why it is wrong:** Names, phone numbers, addresses, and data that identifies or can identify a customer — including order history or payment references where linked to an identifiable person — may constitute personal data under PDPL. The E-Commerce Law does not displace PDPL.
- **Better answer:** "Customer phone numbers, addresses, order history, and payment references may constitute personal data where they identify or can identify a customer. PDPL may apply in parallel with the E-Commerce Law and should be analyzed under its own framework."

### Example 6 — "Selling through social-media DMs is outside any e-commerce rules."

- **Bad answer:** "Social-media selling is informal and is therefore automatically outside the E-Commerce Law."
- **Why it is wrong:** Whether a given activity falls within the Law depends on the **activity, parties, platform model, and consumer-facing flow** — not on the choice of channel. Assuming a blanket exemption is incorrect.
- **Better answer:** "Social-media selling is not automatically outside the E-Commerce Law. Applicability should be checked against the Law, its implementing regulation, and Ministry of Commerce guidance, based on the actual activity, the parties, the platform model, the consumer-facing terms, and the payment/delivery flow. The specific situation should be reviewed with a licensed Saudi lawyer."
